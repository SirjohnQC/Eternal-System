# Tech cards (TP): design

*2026-10-14. Issue #40 ("reaching TP should bring new cards to spend on research
and technologies"). The first card, A NEW WORLD, shipped in #124/#125.*

## What a tech card is

Faith Cards (DP) are signs from a god: nations may heed them or not. **Tech cards
are what your people decide to do with what they know.** They are spent from TP,
the knowledge your people have gathered, and they always happen, but only
where the knowledge is: a tech card acts on the nations that know its technology.
Knowledge spreads unevenly (trade, contact, war, theft), so the same card lifts
one nation and leaves its neighbour behind. That gap is itself a pressure
(envy, rivalry, war).

Each card follows the vision's rule for interventions (§13): **benefit + cost +
risk + long-term consequence**. Nothing is a flat bonus. A card is an
*operator on the state* (the same omen operators Faith Cards use: food,
materials, research, growth, death, cohesion, attitude, pollution...) with a
**price** riding along or following after. The story is what the nations do
with it: a harvest boom fills cradles, crowded towns press on the land, the
land runs short, and someone marches on the neighbour.

## Discovery (§11)

A card appears in the hand when **any of your nations learns its technology**
(the toast names it). Cards whose technology no one knows yet show as a sealed
count per age ("3 more in the Industrial age"). Interstellar cards also need
what they act on (a colony to reach, a polity to talk to).

## TP economy

| Source | TP |
|---|---|
| Your world enters a new age | +3 (as before) |
| **A technology learned for the first time** by any of your nations | **+1** (new) |

About 48 technologies and 8 ages: **~70 TP over a whole game**, ~6–9 per age.
Cards cost 2–12. A player can play most early cards once and has to choose late.

**Wear:** a card played recently costs 25% more per recent use, fading over
about two ages of play (like Faith Cards, "the heavens tire"). Spamming one card
gets expensive.

## Rules the build follows

- **Engine methods only** (rule: new player actions go through the engine). The UI
  lists cards and asks for a choice where one is needed (A New World, Remake a World).
- **Deterministic:** every card plays through a forked RNG.
- **Chronicle:** each play is a player entry ("You spent 4 TP on Great Canals"),
  and every omen it lays links back to it, so *"I caused this 800 years ago"*
  can be walked.
- **Saved:** card wear and timed effects (defence, kinship) live in optional
  `gameState` fields; older saves load with none.
- **Guards:** a `tools/techCardsCheck.ts` plays every card in a seeded world and
  checks it took effect where it should and nowhere else, its price was laid,
  TP were spent, and wear raised the next price; invariant rules for the TP
  economy.
